About

I am a computational biologist with a PhD in Biochemistry. I received extensive training in wet-lab molecular biology, but over the course of my PhD, my research gradually shifted toward bioinformatics, which ultimately became my main scientific focus and passion.

My work centers on the analysis of high-dimensional biological data, with particular expertise in single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics.

I currently work as a freelancer, providing support in single-cell multi-omics data analysis. One notable project I am involved in is Nurture, a Digital Health Accelerator (DHA)-funded project (Stage 2), where I contribute computational expertise toward the development of an AI-based platform aimed at translating molecular and clinical data into actionable solutions for preeclampsia.

In addition, I provide bioinformatics support to the AG Müller/Dechend group at the Max Delbrück Center for Molecular Medicine (MDC).

Background

I hold a degree in Biotechnology and Chemical Engineering (ChemBiotech) from the École Supérieure de Biotechnologie de Strasbourg (ESBS) and the École européenne d’ingénieurs en Chimie, Polymères et Matériaux (ECPM). During my studies, I developed a strong interest in single-cell technologies through multiple research internships.

At the Helmholtz Institute for RNA-based Infection Research (HIRI) in Würzburg, I worked under the supervision of Dr. Saliba, focusing on the implementation of a wet-lab protocol for single-bacterium RNA sequencing.

I then completed another internship at the Columbia University Irving Medical Center (CUIMC) in Dr. Han’s lab in New York, where I contributed to establishing sample multiplexing strategies later used to link distinct T cell states to clinical prognosis in colorectal cancer patients.

Following this, I pursued my PhD at the Berlin Institute for Medical Systems Biology (BIMSB) within the Genomics Technology Platform. My doctoral research focused on adrenal gland biology, combining single-nucleus and spatial transcriptomics to study both development and disease.

This work was structured around two complementary projects. The first was conducted in collaboration with Dr. Barbara Altieri, Dr. Cristina Ronchi, and Dr. Martin Fassnacht from the University Hospital Würzburg (UKW), where I investigated the cellular and molecular heterogeneity of inactive and cortisol-producing adrenal adenomas.

The second project was performed in collaboration with Dr. Ute I. Scholl at the Berlin Institute of Health (BIH), focusing on mapping adrenal gland development in mice and profiling cell types within human aldosterone-producing adenomas (manuscript in preparation).
